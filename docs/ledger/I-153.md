### I-153 · On the Claude Code backend, T0's tool-call cap was the coder's turn cap, so every T0 session was cut off mid-reconnaissance
- Found: 2026-11-04 · issue #79, the graphcalc live run's S9 · P8 attempt 1 (`build-graphcalc-m2-p8-a1`, T0) halted `exit_reason: stall` after five coder sessions of 18, 22, 10, 20 and 14 tool calls, four of which edited nothing
- Symptom: 8 of the run's 9 T0 coder sessions ended `error_max_turns` at 10 turns, 5 before any edit; all 8 documenter sessions ended at 12 turns, 2 before any edit ("Stopped mid-investigation, before making any documentation edits")
- Root cause: `make_worker` passed `toolbox.max_calls or max_model_calls` as `ClaudeCodeWorker(max_turns=...)`, so `T0_TOOL_CAP = 10` (tool calls per iteration in SCIO's `AgentLoop`) became `--max-turns 10`, and the documenter's `max_calls=12` became `--max-turns 12`. A Claude Code turn is one assistant message (the session log's `max_turns_reached` fires at `turnCount 11` after 10 message ids) carrying 1.6 tool calls at T0, and a fresh T0 session, with no plan or localize summary, needs about 14 to 16. ADR 0012 decision 2 wrote the reuse down. Same class as I-099: a guard calibrated in one backend's unit, reused in another's.
- Fix: measured in the graphcalc run (the cost ledgers joined by session id to Claude Code's session logs, read-only): P1 needed 14 turns, P8 attempt 2 20 over two sessions; 17 uncapped T1 coder sessions used 11 to 29 (median 19); a 17 to 22-turn session cost a median $0.52. `T0_TURN_CAP = 20` (`scio/graph/run_context.py`) and `DOCUMENTER_TURN_CAP = 20` (`scio/loop/backends.py`), with the numbers in their docstrings; `claude_code_max_turns` is the one place a SCIO cap becomes `--max-turns`. SCIO's own loop keeps 10 tool calls; T1/T2 keep 30 turns; the explorer keeps 8. Both caps are estimates from sessions cut off at the old caps: recheck after the next live T0 run on this backend.
